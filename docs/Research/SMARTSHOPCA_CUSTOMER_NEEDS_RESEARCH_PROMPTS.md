# SmartShopCA — Customer Needs Research Prompts

> **Prepared:** 2026-10-13
> **Purpose:** Copy-ready prompts for external deep research into customer needs, alternatives, and counter-evidence
> **Founder context:** Side income first, possible income replacement later, optional software expansion; approximately 14 hours per week; bootstrap with justified spending
> **Status:** Research instructions. Findings require source checking and customer testing before they become project decisions.

## How to use

Run each prompt in the named tool's Deep Research mode, using a fresh chat. Each prompt includes its own context. The assignments divide the work; they do not imply that one model's conclusions are authoritative.

Bring back the complete reports with direct source links and dates. We will compare the evidence, select a reachable customer group, and design the first purchase test. Keep the seasonal and skincare findings distinct.

Related records: [first-pass business review](BUSINESS_PLAN_FIRST_PASS_REVIEW_2026-10-13.md) and [continuation handoff](SOL_BUSINESS_PLAN_NEXT_STEPS_HANDOFF.md).

## 1. ChatGPT — Seasonal products and Canadian gifting

```text
Research whether a real customer opportunity exists for SmartShopCA's
“Seasonal Canadian Favourites” offering.

CONTEXT
SmartShopCA is a proposed Canadian product-discovery and affiliate service.
It would curate small selections of worthwhile, distinctive products,
with clear purchasing information and transparent recommendations.

The founder wants side income first, possible income replacement later,
and optional software expansion if demand supports it. Available time:
approximately 14 hours per week. Bootstrap wherever possible; justify
spending through learning or revenue potential.

Do not assume that Canadians need another shopping website.

RESEARCH QUESTION
Which specific Canadian purchasing situations involve unresolved
difficulty—or valuable discovery, gifting, or curation needs—that a
small independent service could realistically address?

Investigate candidates such as:
- Canadian-made gifts within a budget and delivery deadline;
- distinctive seasonal products from Canadian makers;
- practical seasonal purchases requiring Canadian availability,
  shipping, returns, or regional context.

Do not limit your findings to these examples.

EVIDENCE
Use Canadian public statistics, relevant surveys, Reddit, RedFlagDeals,
retailer reviews/questions, and existing services.

Prefer recent evidence from the last 24 months. Older evidence is allowed
when useful, but label its age and limitations. Distinguish Canadian-owned,
Canadian-made, and merely available in Canada.

For each candidate need, identify:
1. Exact customer and purchasing trigger.
2. What people actually experienced or did.
3. Their existing solution and where it succeeds or fails.
4. Consequences: time, expense, disappointment, abandoned purchases,
   or difficulty discovering suitable products.
5. What SmartShopCA could contribute.
6. Counter-evidence and reasons the opportunity might be weak.
7. A feasible way to reach these buyers.

Separate representative statistics, individual anecdotes, and inference.
Deduplicate repeated stories and syndicated sources. Post counts and
upvotes do not establish prevalence or market size.

OUTPUT
Return Markdown containing:
- A candid verdict: supported, promising but unproven, or weak.
- Up to five specific customer needs, ranked with explained uncertainty.
- An evidence table with direct links, dates, geography, observed
  behaviour, and limitations.
- Existing alternatives for the strongest needs.
- Two inexpensive validation experiments within 28 founder-hours total.
- Interview questions about actual recent purchases.
- The evidence that would make you reject your recommendation.

Do not invent source access, statistics, commissions, or demand.
Avoid a broad business plan or speculative five-year forecasts.
```

## 2. Gemini — Canadian skincare purchasing needs

```text
Research whether Canadian skincare buyers have unresolved purchasing
needs that SmartShopCA could responsibly address.

CONTEXT
SmartShopCA is a proposed Canadian product-discovery and comparison
service. Its skincare offering would explain product origin, declared
ingredients, sourcing, and relevant limitations through a transparent
methodology.

The founder wants side income first, possible income replacement later,
and optional software expansion if demand supports it. Available time:
approximately 14 hours per week. Bootstrap wherever possible.

This research concerns buying decisions and information needs.
Do not provide diagnosis, treatment advice, or individualized safety
verdicts. Do not assume “clean,” “natural,” or “Canadian” means safer.

RESEARCH QUESTION
Which specific skincare purchasing decisions do Canadian buyers
struggle with, and are existing resources already adequate?

Investigate:
- finding products genuinely manufactured in Canada;
- distinguishing manufacturing from Canadian ownership or formulation;
- comparing products against declared ingredient preferences;
- finding accurate current ingredient lists;
- understanding product/formula changes;
- choosing among Canadian options within a budget.

Also investigate valuable discovery needs, not just frustration.
Do not assume consumers want an ingredient scanner or subscription.

EVIDENCE
Use Canadian consumer research, relevant Reddit communities, retailer
reviews/questions, existing comparison tools, and primary product sources.
Use Health Canada or appropriate scientific sources when checking
regulatory or ingredient-related factual claims.

Prefer evidence from the last 24 months. Label older evidence.
Treat forum statements about ingredients or brands as reported concerns,
not verified scientific or origin facts.

For each candidate need, identify:
1. Exact buyer and purchase trigger.
2. Actual behaviour and existing workaround.
3. What remains unresolved.
4. Whether the need recurs, and at what observed frequency.
5. Whether better information changes purchasing behaviour.
6. Existing free and paid alternatives.
7. The expertise, verification, and maintenance work required.
8. Counter-evidence.

OUTPUT
Return Markdown containing:
- A candid verdict on the opportunity.
- Up to five narrowly defined customer needs.
- An evidence table with direct links, dates, geography, behaviour,
  evidence type, and limitations.
- A comparison of existing alternatives.
- The two most feasible needs for a solo founder to test.
- A small manual experiment for each, with proposed time requirements.
- Interview questions based on recent purchases.
- Separate conclusions about free consumer usefulness, paid consumer
  demand, and professional/B2B demand.

Deduplicate sources. Do not infer demand from upvotes or AI agreement.
Do not invent source access, credentials, willingness to pay, or
ingredient-safety conclusions. Avoid speculative financial forecasts.
```

## 3. Perplexity — Competitors, counter-evidence, and commercial feasibility

```text
Independently challenge the proposed customer opportunity for SmartShopCA.

CONTEXT
SmartShopCA is a proposed Canadian shopping-discovery and comparison
service with two separate offerings:
1. Seasonal Canadian Favourites: small, curated product selections.
2. Canadian skincare: transparent origin and ingredient information.

Potential value includes worthwhile discovery, Canadian-made products,
cross-merchant comparison, purchasing context, and sourced explanations.

The founder wants side income first, possible income replacement later,
and optional software expansion if recurring demand emerges. Available
time: approximately 14 hours per week. Bootstrap wherever possible.

Your job is to identify where existing solutions already work, where
meaningful gaps remain, and which gaps could support a small business.
Be balanced: neither promote nor reflexively dismiss the idea.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS
1. What existing services solve these customer jobs today?
2. What do buyers still struggle with after using those services?
3. Are the gaps valuable enough to influence actual purchasing?
4. Could a solo founder address them economically?
5. What evidence would distinguish a useful affiliate publication,
   a paid research service, and a recurring software opportunity?

INVESTIGATE
- Canadian product directories and curated publications;
- retailers and merchant shopping tools;
- Shopify Shop and other shopping assistants;
- Reddit and RedFlagDeals;
- skincare ingredient/comparison tools;
- newsletters, creators, and professional resources.

Verify material current capabilities through primary documentation
or direct inspection. Distinguish advertised capability from observed
performance. If practical, test alternatives on a few explicit shopping
tasks and report the task, date, method, and limitations.

Look for customers who are satisfied with current options, as well as
complaints. Identify whether apparently separate sources repeat the same
underlying story.

OUTPUT
Return Markdown containing:
- A candid verdict and strongest argument on both sides.
- A competitor table: customer job, actual capabilities, geographic
  coverage, pricing where verified, and remaining limitations.
- An evidence table of unresolved needs and counter-evidence, with
  direct links and dates.
- Up to three gaps worth testing, ranked with uncertainty explained.
- Feasible customer-acquisition routes for a small pilot.
- A commercial evidence checklist: actual affiliate terms, acquisition
  effort, research/update effort, and payment evidence still needed.
- Clear rejection criteria for each proposed gap.

Do not invent conversion rates, revenues, market sizes, or willingness
to pay. Separate usefulness from defensibility and from monetization.
Do not assume a consumer dataset has B2B buyers or that AI platforms
will license it. Report inaccessible sources and unresolved questions.
```
