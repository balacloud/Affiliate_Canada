# Moisturizer buyer interview kit

Prepared 2026-10-14. Draft for founder review/use; no outreach or interviews conducted. Goal: choose between a face and body moisturizer comparison by observing actual purchase difficulties. This is discovery, not a national seasonal-use survey or a clinical study.

**Founder steering, 2026-10-14:** start with existing community discussions. This interview kit is optional follow-up for unanswered questions, not a prerequisite. See [the community listening first pass](MOISTURIZER_COMMUNITY_LISTENING_2026-10-14.md).

## Small first round

Propose five conversations of approximately 20 minutes with Canadian adults who bought a moisturizer recently or have an upcoming replacement. Include face and body purchasers if available. Ask province and purchase context; do not invent age targeting. Familiar buyers are convenient but introduce sampling bias. Five interviews generate hypotheses, not market prevalence or validation thresholds.

Possible routes: people already reachable personally, or one relevant community that permits research invitations. Group participation and posting need the group's rules and permission where required. A Facebook page or Instagram follower count is not interview evidence. No paid recruitment proposed. This work could fit in a suggested 3–4-hour block including notes and synthesis; that is a planning estimate, not measured delivery time.

## Draft invitation — not sent

> I'm researching how Canadians choose face and body moisturizers for colder weather for SmartShopCA. Have you recently bought one, or are you about to replace yours? I'd like to ask about your actual shopping experience for around 20 minutes. This is product-choice research, not medical advice or a sales pitch. You can skip any question. Would you be interested?

At the start, explain how notes will be used and ask permission to take them. Use anonymous IDs. Do not record audio, collect medical records, or subscribe participants to marketing by default. Ask separately before any later follow-up and retain contact information separately from research notes.

## Interview sequence

1. **Last purchase:** Which exact moisturizer did you last buy, when, where, and for which part of your body? What triggered that purchase?
2. **Existing method:** Walk me through how you chose it. What other options did you consider? Which information or person did you trust?
3. **Cost:** What did you pay, including shipping if known? Did that change which product or merchant you chose?
4. **Season:** What actually changed in your purchases or use as the weather changed last year? What stayed the same? Do not prompt for richer creams before hearing their answer.
5. **Difficulties:** What was difficult to establish? What made you reject an option? Ask about ingredients, scent, texture and origin only after the unaided response.
6. **Origin:** Did manufacturing location affect this purchase? What evidence did you look for? Would the choice have been different without that information?
7. **Outcome:** What happened after buying? Did you use it, replace it, return it, or abandon it? What specifically disappointed you? A participant's report is not a verified efficacy or allergy claim.
8. **Upcoming decision:** Are you choosing another product soon? What would you compare it against, and what information is missing?

Avoid “Would you use our website?” or “Do you care about non-toxic products?” as demand evidence. Do not ask participants to change treatment or experiment on irritated skin.

## Optional comparison exercise

After documenting the existing method, and only with adequate verified product information, show a small neutral comparison. Record which information they used, whether it changed a choice, and what remains missing. Do not expose affiliate rates or use them to influence the shortlist. An expressed preference is weaker evidence than a subsequent purchase or documented decision.

## Note template

Use [the blank interview CSV](MOISTURIZER_INTERVIEW_NOTES.csv). Record anonymous ID, province, category, exact product, purchase trigger/date/channel, reported total cost, existing alternatives, unaided friction, origin relevance, seasonal change, outcome, next decision, permission for follow-up, and recruitment/interview/research minutes. Quote only with consent. Do not enter names, emails, medical diagnoses or invented responses in this tracked file; actual participant notes should be kept in an appropriately private location.

## Review after the first round

Compare face and body separately. Look for repeated concrete friction, a real upcoming purchase, evidence that would improve the current approach, accessible products, and total research/maintenance effort. Preserve contrary cases where current products already work well. Choose a follow-up test based on observed value and reachable buyers, rather than interview counts or compliments. Payment, return use and affiliate conversion remain unmeasured.

The founder's immediate contribution is identifying reachable buyers. Product type, test scope and any cash cap remain pending. No full customer pilot or public launch is claimed by this document.
