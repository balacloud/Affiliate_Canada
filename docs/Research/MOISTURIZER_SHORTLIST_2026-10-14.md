# SmartShopCA — face versus body moisturizer research

Research date and source access date: 2026-10-14. Scope: five exact products from manufacturer sites, merchant policies, and official affiliate pages. This is an editorial research shortlist, not a best-product ranking, medical recommendation, completed safety assessment, or published collection. No hands-on tests, purchases, interviews, applications, outreach, or deployment occurred.

## Decision we can make next

Compare two buying jobs: choosing a face moisturizer versus choosing a body moisturizer for colder weather. Body care currently offers two candidates with directly readable lists and no listed perfume or aromatic essential oils (ATTITUDE and Rocky); origin evidence still needs qualification. Face care offers useful price contrast, but Marcelle origin and Riversol's current full formula remain unresolved. This convenience sample cannot establish market size or which category Canadians use most.

Proposed discovery cohort: reachable Canadian adults who recently bought or are about to replace a face/body moisturizer and care about at least one of origin, ingredient clarity, fragrance, texture, or total cost. These preferences must be observed, not presumed. Do not impose an unsupported age band. See [buyer interview kit](MOISTURIZER_BUYER_INTERVIEW_KIT_2026-10-14.md).

## Exact-product findings and limitations

### Riversol

[Manufacturer product page](https://www.riversol.com/products/daily-moisturizing-cream) identifies the 50 mL cream, fragrance-free and Canadian-made claims. It cites a 22-person, 28-day trial; full study methods/results were not obtained. This is attributed brand evidence, not independent superiority. Current page/HTML exposed key ingredients but no complete list. A historic holiday bundle search result is not a current formula baseline. The page mixes notification and purchase controls; retrieved structured data says InStock. Recheck the exact variant before a live stock claim. Choose-if: buyer wants a face-cream comparison and accepts this price. Avoid-if: full formula is required before considering it; do not present it as fully screened yet.

### Marcelle

[Manufacturer product page](https://marcelle.com/en/products/moisture-cream-50ml?variant=44454916423879) publishes the formula and fragrance-free claim; glycerin, niacinamide, sodium hyaluronate and dimethicone are disclosed. No parfum/fragrance or aromatic essential oils were identified in that published list; this is a list observation, not an allergy assessment. The page warns formulas can change. Canadian storefront HTML lists CAD $22.95 and InStock. [Brand origin statement](https://marcelle.com/en/pages/notre-engagement-envers-vous) covers 89% of skincare, not this exact SKU. Choose-if: lower-price face-cream candidate with readable ingredients. Avoid-if: Canadian manufacture must already be established for the exact item.

### ATTITUDE

[Manufacturer product page](https://ca.attitudeliving.com/products/body-lotion-sensitive-skin/) specifies the Unscented 473 mL variant, no-added-fragrance claim, and ingredient list, including glycerin, oat kernel flour, shea butter and coconut oil. No parfum/fragrance or aromatic essential oils were identified in that list. Packaging is stated to be more accurate when formulas evolve. Certification/dermatological claims have not been independently checked. Purchase control is visible; live variant fulfilment is untested. [Factory page](https://ca.attitudeliving.com/our-factory) describes a Canadian facility, without an exact-SKU manufacturing record. Choose-if: buyer wants a large-format body lotion candidate with transparent formula information. Avoid-if: a known personal ingredient exclusion or exact-origin uncertainty rules it out; no universal suitability claim.

### Green Beaver

[Manufacturer product page](https://greenbeaver.com/products/intense-moisture-body-lotion) calls the product made in Canada. Its narrative describes an unscented/no-added-fragrance product while the published list includes rosemary, lavender and cinnamon bark oils, cinnamal, limonene, eugenol and linalool. Show this conflict; do not recast it as a medical verdict. Static content includes both sold-out and ready-to-ship text; availability is unresolved. Broad ingredient-danger, healing, and certification claims are brand statements, not adopted findings. Choose-if: researching how buyers interpret scent claims. Avoid-if: the proposed comparison requires no disclosed aromatic essential oils. It is not an established fragrance-free fit.

### Rocky Mountain Soap

[Manufacturer product page](https://www.rockymountainsoap.com/products/unscented-body-butter) lists sunflower seed oil, sesame seed oil, beeswax, cocoa butter and tocopherol. No parfum/fragrance or aromatic essential oils are disclosed. Beeswax makes it unsuitable for a vegan criterion; ingredient simplicity is not a safety guarantee. The product-page manufacture statement is Canadian Rockies; label/batch evidence remains pending. Online purchase control exists, but store stock lookup failed. Do not promise local store availability. Choose-if: a buyer is open to a solid body-butter format and beeswax. Avoid-if: vegan requirement, a personal exclusion, or preference for a pump lotion makes it unsuitable. Texture/performance statements have not been tested by SmartShopCA.

## Evidence method v0.1 — factual review only

This working method records origin claim scope, ingredient-disclosure availability, declared scent claims, listed parfum/fragrance, aromatic oils, disclosed fragrance allergens, format, merchant terms, and evidence gaps. Missing input is unknown. It does not assign a toxic/non-toxic score, assess every chemical, verify regulatory compliance, or establish that an absent listed ingredient is absent from the physical product.

The proposed no-aromatic-oils comparison criterion is a preference-based filter, not an approved medical methodology. No concentrations, formula identifiers or physical labels were obtained. No product receives a complete ingredient-screening pass or endocrine-safety verdict. [Health Canada explains fragrance/unscented terms, hypoallergenic limits, and ingredient disclosure](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/cosmetics/cosmetic-advertising-labelling-ingredients.html); [its labelling guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/labelling-cosmetics.html) provides the fragrance-allergen context. These sources do not establish individual tolerance.
