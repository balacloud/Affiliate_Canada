## Context

SmartShopCA is a Canadian discovery and affiliate project. Its selected first launch pillar is Canadian-made personal care and household products, with skincare as the first category. Household products and a separate Seasonal Canadian Favourites pillar are future options. Seasonal skincare stays within the personal-care pillar. ## Research questions

1. Which skincare categories do Canadians use regularly, buy most often, or spend the most on? Separate these three measures. Examine facial cleansers, face moisturizers, body lotions, hand creams, lip care, sunscreen, serums, and other categories where evidence supports inclusion. Do not force a ranking when comparable data is absent.
2. What changes across autumn, winter, spring, and summer? Distinguish measured seasonal purchases/use/searches from clinical guidance and inferred needs. Identify province/climate, age, skin concern, and other audience differences only where supported. Do not assume Canada is one climate or that everyone changes products each season.
3. What concrete buying problems recur: fragrance, texture, price, unclear ingredients, uncertain manufacturing origin, shipping, availability, or disappointment with a prior product? Use observed behaviour where available; identify recruitment routes for further interviews without contacting anyone.
4. For the strongest category candidates, are there Canadian-manufactured products with adequate ingredient disclosure and accessible Canadian merchants? Check exact products/variants and qualifying origin language. Canadian ownership does not establish Canadian manufacturing. Separate initial candidates from verified products, and mark unconfirmed manufacturing explicitly.
5. Which up to three narrow buyer-and-purchase opportunities deserve testing first for an autumn/winter launch? Compare demand evidence, decision difficulty, accessible buyers, verifiable supply, research/update effort, and monetization feasibility. Do not claim paid demand or affiliate eligibility without evidence.

## Evidence rules

Use current Canadian evidence, prioritizing approximately the last three years. Older evidence is acceptable when clearly dated and its limitations explained. Prefer original research providers, Statistics Canada, Health Canada, the Canadian Dermatology Association, primary scientific research, and exact manufacturer/merchant documentation.

For each important claim provide the direct source URL, publication date, access date, geography, population/sample, and metric when available. Open sources and check that they support the claim. Paywalled previews are not access to the underlying dataset. Do not fabricate missing figures or describe sources you did not access as verified.

Separate nationwide usage from prestige retail sales, revenue from unit volume, growth from category size, searches from purchases, and recommendations from actual behaviour. Community posts are qualitative examples, not representative prevalence estimates. Distinguish purchases by Canadians from purchases of Canadian-made products. Avoid turning “natural” or “hypoallergenic” into a safety guarantee.

Label conclusions as directly supported finding, attributed commercial claim, inference, hypothesis, or unknown. Include contrary evidence and existing alternatives. Multiple models agreeing does not establish demand.
