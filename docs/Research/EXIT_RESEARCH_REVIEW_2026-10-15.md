# Returned exit research — critical review

Date: October 15, 2026. Founder supplied an outside-research report after requesting an acquisition-oriented research prompt. This is a targeted audit of its central conclusions and selected sources, not verification of every cited transaction. No roadmap, spending, outreach or build expansion authorized by the report.

## Verdict and strongest case

Proceed with the focused publication and reusable product evidence. Explore a narrowly scoped paid evidence service if it addresses a demonstrated buyer problem. Founder wants the option to sell after several years; build transferable operations now without treating a sale as assured. Do not adopt a deterministic 3–5-year ban on software. Model transitions should follow customer evidence and delivery economics.

## Verified and corrected findings

- [Empire Flippers analysis](https://empireflippers.com/you-can-sell-your-business-for-life-changing-money-in-2025-heres-the-secret/) reports 145 content-site transactions across 2023 through roughly May 2025, not a complete 2025 or current 2026 market dataset. It supports the existence of content-site sales; it does not establish a SmartShopCA valuation or acquisition probability. Broker data is commercially interested and selected from its own marketplace.
- [Flippa's guide](https://flippa.com/blog/affiliate-website-valuation-how-to-properly-determine-it/) cites 30–45 times monthly net profit and separately 3–6 times annual SDE. These are different ranges and potentially different earnings bases. On the same earnings basis, 30–45 monthly equals 2.5–3.75 annual, not 3–6. Do not combine revenue, net profit, SDE and EBITDA multiples. Broker guidance is not equivalent to a disclosed comparable sale.
- The report's illustrative 2–4 times annual earnings calculations are arithmetically correct, but the chosen multiple is not validated for SmartShopCA. Applying a negotiation discount to a value already based on completed-sale multiples can double-count the reduction. Distinguish asking value, agreed consideration, cash paid at closing, contingent consideration, fees and taxes. No net-proceeds estimate adopted.
- [Storeware's announcement](https://startise.com/storeware-acquires-easyflow-shopify-app/) is dated November 5, 2024 and describes EasyFlow as a product-options app, not workflow automation. It confirms an acquisition with no usable disclosed price/profit multiple. This contradicts the report's claim that only affiliate businesses have strong small-business acquisition precedent when the report itself also cites apps; quality of matched comparables, not an exclusive category claim, matters.
- [Rokt's AfterSell announcement](https://www.rokt.com/blog/rokt-to-acquire-aftersell-to-expand-smb-offering-and-unlock-more-relevant-experiences-for-shopify-customers) supports a strategic acquisition, not a directly comparable small-founder valuation. Enterprise transactions cannot fill the missing micro-data-business evidence.

## Unsupported assumptions and contradictions

- “No verified traffic/revenue/subscribers” should not become “confirmed zero.” Actual analytics and accounts have not been audited.
- “50–200 SKUs is feasible at 14 hours/week” lacks measured refresh time, source count, exception rate and support load. Time the workflow first.
- Three to five pages, 10–20 pages and adding household categories are proposed outputs, not proven launch requirements. Current scope remains two moisturizer comparisons; both top-level pillars remain distinct.
- “Two to three years of earnings required” is not a universal transaction prerequisite. Longer history may help underwriting; buyer/broker requirements vary.
- Models C and F overlap: product-evidence intelligence is an outcome; manual report, monitoring subscription and API are delivery/commercial forms. They need not become separate businesses.
- The report's publication-model table confuses the future acquirer with the current customer. Consumer job: choose a product with clearer evidence. Affiliate merchant job: obtain attributable qualified purchases. Acquirer job: buy transferable earnings/assets. Prove consumer usefulness and merchant economics before optimizing for acquisition.
- Unpaid sample acceptance is not willingness to pay. One payment is not retention. Commission income repeating monthly is not contracted subscription revenue.
- Citations need claim-level matching: a currency page does not support access-control recommendations. The cited spot FX rate is unverified and unnecessary for CAD-only scenario arithmetic.

## Economics and founder reality

Fourteen hours/week averages approximately 60.7 hours/month. At an illustrative founder-selected opportunity cost of CAD 50/hour, labour represents approximately CAD 3,033/month. This rate is a sensitivity assumption, not market evidence. A business reporting CAD 3,000 monthly benefit before owner compensation can have little economic surplus after that labour allowance. Keep owner-operator cash benefit and profit after replacement labour separate; avoid subtracting replacement labour twice if already expensed.

Maintenance cost depends on exact variants × source pages × refresh frequency × minutes per check, plus exceptions, review, support and sales. No feasible SKU count, margin or acquisition value has been measured.

Finish one useful face and one body comparison, verify real audience/affiliate outcomes, and time evidence creation plus repeat refresh. Preserve source/variant history and document operations. If a defined business buyer values the same records, test one narrow paid report before developing a dashboard, app or API. Do not require a calendar wait before responding to paid recurring demand.

For exit readiness, track actual revenue/costs, founder hours, acquisition-channel and merchant concentration, account/contract transfer restrictions, content/code/data rights and the work another operator must perform. A source-linked dataset may support trust without automatically becoming a licensable moat.

Editorial independence remains essential: brands cannot buy positive placement, evidence passes or suppression of documented concerns. Payment for service is separate from listing/corrections. Data licensing and transaction-specific legal/tax matters require appropriate review when concrete.
