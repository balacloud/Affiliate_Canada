# Canadian skincare seasonal research — returned report review

Reviewed 2026-10-14. Source: founder-pasted outside-LLM report; provider/model not identified. This is a condensed review, not a verbatim archive or validation of all report claims. No product type, pilot, spending, or publication approved through this review.

## Decision usefulness

Moisturizers for colder-weather buying decisions are a reasonable research candidate. Sunscreen is another candidate with measured Canadian usage evidence. Neither is proven to be SmartShopCA's best business opportunity. No credible national usage/purchase ranking across seasons was established by the checked sources; this is a finding about this research, not proof that no such dataset exists.

## Checked claims

- Statistics Canada confirms 2023 household personal-care spending of $1,860, up 30.1% from 2021, and a 66.2% increase in the combined makeup/skin-care/manicure/fragrance category. These are current-dollar expenditures, not skincare-only growth or unit purchases. Canada-level estimates cover provinces, not territories. [2023 SHS release](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250521/dq250521a-eng.htm).
- Ipsos/CDA confirms 65% at least occasional sunscreen use when outdoors, 28% year-round use, 19% daily use, and 28% agreement that winter protection is unnecessary. Survey fielded November 10–14, 2023; 1,051 respondents aged 16+. This is self-report/attitudes, not monthly purchases or a winter-use count. 65% is below two-thirds; it cannot be combined with a differently defined commercial measure to assert a common prevalence. [Primary survey factum](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2024-05/CDA%20Sun%20Awareness%20Study%202024%20-%20Factum_0.pdf).
- CDA identifies cold weather, low humidity, and indoor heating as contributors to dry skin and discusses fragrance-free moisturization. This supports the seasonal clinical rationale, not measured sales or proof that a specific product works. [Dry-skin guidance](https://dermatology.ca/public-patients/diseases-conditions/skin-conditions/dry-skin/).

## Material corrections

1. **Winter spike arithmetic:** The IndexBox page does contain the claimed >85% penetration, repurchase intervals, and 30–40% annual volume in October–February. But five months represent about 41.7% of a year under a simple equal-month baseline. A share of 30–40% therefore does not establish the report's pronounced winter spike. Monthly distribution could still contain a peak, but the cited share alone cannot prove it. The original report's “verified” labels overstate confidence in commercial assertions whose underlying evidence was not independently examined. [Commercial page](https://www.indexbox.io/store/canada-kw-body-lotion-moisturizing-840-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/).
2. **Skin protection is not sunscreen:** Mintel's public 2026 summary states 72% use skin-protection products. Its category includes sunless tanners, tanning lotions/oils, and personal-care products with protection claims, including anti-pollution. It is not a sunscreen-only usage figure. [Mintel summary and definitions](https://store.mintel.com/report/canada-suncare-and-skin-protection-market-report).
3. **Natural/organic prevalence unresolved:** The supplied Mintel natural/organic page failed to open in this review, and targeted search did not establish the 78% figure. Mark it unconfirmed; access failure does not establish that it is false. Even an accurate broad personal-care prevalence would not establish demand for Canadian-made skincare with transparent screening.
4. **Product verification overstated:** Most supplied candidates cite directories and brand-level manufacturing locations, not exact product/variant labels. Treat them as discovery leads. Do not blanket-label named brands imported or Canadian-manufactured. Manufacture, ownership, certifications, disclosed formula, and efficacy require separate checks. “Not Product of Canada” cannot be inferred merely from missing ingredient-origin detail.
5. **No representative category ranking:** A search that fails to locate an open ranking does not prove none exists. Neither guidance nor product availability ranks actual use. Age/region targeting and superiority of body over face moisturizers remain hypotheses.

## Exact-product spot check

ATTITUDE's official Sensitive Skin Body Lotion page identifies an unscented variant, says no added fragrance, and discloses an ingredient list. It warns packaging is the most accurate list because formulas evolve. This establishes available manufacturer information, not a completed origin check, ingredient-screening pass, certification verification, or efficacy assessment. [Manufacturer page](https://ca.attitudeliving.com/products/body-lotion-sensitive-skin/).

## Proportionate next research

Compare face and body moisturizer candidates using exact-product origin, formula disclosure, credible performance evidence, current merchant details, total cost, and affiliate availability. Pair this with a small number of behaviour-based buyer conversations when outreach is authorized. Do not invent age cutoffs or require a national ranking before a bounded test.

The suggested 20–40-person, 4–6-week diary plus 20–30-product audit is a substantial workload for 14 hours/week. It cannot establish national seasonality without appropriate design and follow-up across seasons. Retail rank, price, availability, and Google Trends are separate proxies; none directly establishes sales, repeat purchase, or paid demand.
